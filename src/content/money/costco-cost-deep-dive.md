While the market spent 2026 obsessing over AI infrastructure, Costco quietly did what it always does: grow revenue every single month, collect its membership fees, and open warehouses. FY2026 revenue rose 10.1% to $303.2 billion and net income rose 13.9% to $9.2 billion — no GPUs required.

This deep dive covers four questions: Can Costco navigate any economic environment? How insulated is it from an AI bubble? How durable is the membership model? And how much room is left to grow globally? Then a reverse DCF to test what the $445 billion market cap already assumes.

## What Costco does

Costco runs membership-only warehouse clubs: no-frills, concrete-floor stores selling a deliberately narrow range of bulk goods at markups capped barely above cost. Gross margin is about 12.8% — a fraction of a typical retailer's — because the merchandise is not really the business. The business is the membership fee: customers pay $65 a year (Gold Star) or $130 a year (Executive) for access to those low prices, and that fee income is nearly pure profit.

That structure inverts normal retail economics. Costco earns thin-to-zero margin on what it sells and makes its money up front, annually, from members who renew at rates most subscription businesses would envy. It is the third-largest retailer in the world, number 12 on the Fortune 500, the world's largest retailer of beef, poultry, organic produce, and wine — and its Kirkland Signature private label alone accounts for roughly a third of sales.

## FY2026 by the numbers

- **Revenue:** $303.15B, +10.1% YoY (FY25: $275.24B)
- **Net income:** $9.23B, +13.9%; EPS $20.76, +14.0%
- **Membership fee income:** $5.91B, +11.0% — more than half of operating income, at near-100% margin
- **Segment mix:** Foods & Sundries $116.8B; Non-Foods $77.4B; Fresh Foods $41.7B; Ancillary & Other (incl. gas) $61.4B; membership fees $5.9B
- **Margins:** gross 12.8%, operating 3.8%, net 3.0% — thin by design
- **Cash generation:** operating cash flow $15.8B; capex $6.4B; free cash flow $9.38B, +19.7%
- **Balance sheet:** $21.3B cash and investments vs. $10.2B debt — $11.1B net cash
- **Dividend:** $5.54/share paid in FY26 (+12.6%), plus a history of large special dividends ($15/share in January 2024)
- **Consistency:** net sales grew every month of FY26, between +8.5% and +14.5% YoY; digitally enabled sales grew +17% to +34% every month

## Built for any economy

Costco's four-decade record is a study in countercyclical resilience. In FY2009, the worst consumer year in decades, sales dipped only about 1.5% before rebounding double digits the following year. During COVID, pantry-loading sent revenue up 9% in FY2020 and 17.5% in FY2021. During the 2022-24 inflation spike, squeezed households traded down into bulk value, and Costco kept growing.

The 2025-26 tariff shock is the latest test, and Costco's response shows the playbook: it sued the U.S. government in November 2025 seeking full refunds of tariffs imposed under IEEPA, and when $184 million in refunds arrived in 2026, it reinvested most of the money into *lower prices* for members — produce, meat, beverages, home furnishings, hardware. Competitors protect margin; Costco protects the membership value proposition, because the fee is the profit.

There's also a K-shaped-economy angle: analysts read Costco's results as a window into consumer resilience, and its member base skews toward higher-income households that keep spending even when the bottom of the K struggles. Value perception plus an affluent member base is a difficult combination to hurt.

## The anti-AI-bubble stock

Costco's capital program is entirely physical. FY2026 capex was $6.4B — about 2.1% of revenue — spent on warehouses, logistics, and store automation. It owns no GPU fleets, builds no data centers, and sells nothing whose demand depends on AI monetization. If the AI infrastructure buildout stalls or AI-linked valuations deflate, nothing in Costco's income statement is directly exposed. Its drivers are inventory turnover, membership counts, and warehouse openings.

This matters now because so much of the S&P 500's 2026 valuation is tied to the AI capex cycle. Costco is one of the few mega-caps whose earnings power is genuinely orthogonal to it.

The catch: investors have noticed. The October 2026 rotation out of tech and into staples — the "fleeing tech for toothpaste" trade — has bid up exactly the safe names you'd hide in. Costco's insulation from an AI bust is real, but the price of that insulation is not cheap, as the valuation section shows.

## The membership flywheel

The numbers behind the loyalty engine:

- **Fee income:** $5.91B in FY26, +11.0%, growing faster than merchandise revenue
- **Paid members:** 81.4M as of February 2026, +5% YoY; 145.9M total cardholders
- **Renewal rates:** historically ~92-93% in the US/Canada and ~90% worldwide; they dipped slightly in FY26, which management attributes to a growing share of digital sign-ups (newer digital cohorts renew slower)
- **Executive tier:** roughly half of paid members, driving about three-quarters of sales; executive memberships grew 9.6% in Q3 FY26

The pricing lever is pulled with remarkable discipline: membership fees rise roughly every seven years. September 2024 brought the first hike since June 2017 — Gold Star from $60 to $65, Executive from $120 to $130, and the Executive reward cap from $1,000 to $1,250 — affecting 52 million members. Because the fee is almost pure profit and churn is minimal, each hike drops nearly straight to the bottom line over the following two years.

Costco has also been tightening who captures the value: QR and barcode scanners at warehouse entrances to stop card sharing, a members-only food court, and Executive-only 9 a.m. shopping hours at select locations since June 2025. Each move nudges free-riders into paying and members into upgrading. The flywheel is simple: fees fund low prices, low prices justify fees, and renewal makes it recurring.

## Global growth runway

Costco operates 924 warehouses across 14 countries, and the footprint is strikingly concentrated: about 85% of locations are in North America — 634 in the US and Puerto Rico, 114 in Canada, 42 in Mexico.

The rest of the map shows how much runway remains: Japan (37), the UK (29), South Korea (20), Australia (15), and Taiwan (14) are proven, compounding markets — Taiwan, Korea, and the UK were repeatedly named among the strongest comp performers in FY26. Then comes the frontier: China has just 7 warehouses, Spain 5, France 3, Sweden 2, Iceland and New Zealand 1 each. China alone, where the 2019 Shanghai opening drew such crowds the store closed within hours, could eventually support dozens.

Costco opens roughly 24-29 net new warehouses a year — a deliberate ~3% unit growth pace. Against a $445B valuation the pace may look modest, but it is high-quality growth: new warehouses are productive almost immediately, international markets have decades of white space, and every new warehouse seeds another pool of renewing members.

## Bull case

- **All-weather compounding:** revenue grew every month of FY26 through tariff and macro noise; the model has compounded through every regime for four decades.
- **Fee pricing power:** the ~7-year hike cadence plus 5% member growth compounds high-margin fee income at ~10%+ with minimal churn.
- **International runway:** 85% of warehouses are in North America; China and Europe are barely scratched.
- **Balance sheet:** $11.1B net cash, $9.4B FCF growing ~20%, dividends growing ~12-13% with occasional specials.

## Bear case

- **Valuation:** 48x trailing earnings for a retailer growing revenue ~10% — see the reverse DCF below.
- **Saturation risk:** North American maturity could drag growth toward 3-4%, leaving the multiple hard to defend.
- **Renewal drift:** the FY26 dip in renewal rates, driven by lower-quality digital sign-ups, is worth watching; fees are the profit engine.
- **Thin-margin exposure:** 12.8% gross margins leave little room for cost shocks, wage pressure, or a price war with Walmart/Sam's or BJ's.

## Valuation: a reverse DCF

Costco at $1,004.80 (October 26, 2026) is a $445B market cap — 48.4x trailing and 44x forward earnings. What does that price assume? Working backwards with a 10-year model: base FY2026 revenue of $303.15B at its actual 3.1% FCF margin, 443M shares, $11.1B of net cash (so ~$434B of enterprise value), a 10% discount rate, and 3% terminal growth:

| Scenario | 10-yr revenue path | FY36 FCF margin | Implied EV | Per share | vs. $1,005 today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear** | 10% decaying to 4% | 3.0% | ~$184B | ~$439 | ~−56% |
| **Bull** | 9% for 5 yrs, then 6% | 4.5% | ~$266B | ~$625 | ~−38% |
| **Aggressive bull** | 10% for 5 yrs, then 7% | 5.5% | ~$341B | ~$796 | ~−21% |
| **Aggressive bull (9% discount)** | same | 5.5% | ~$408B | ~$946 | ~−6% |
| **Market-implied** | **~14% every year** | 4.5% | ~$434B | ~$1,005 | — |

The finding: even the aggressive bull case — a full decade of growth *above* anything Costco has recently delivered, with free-cash-flow margins nearly doubling — lands about 20% below today's price. To justify $445B, you need revenue to compound at roughly **14% a year for ten years**, reaching ~$1.13 trillion by FY36. Costco's actual revenue CAGR over the last four fiscal years was 7.5%; its EPS CAGR was 12.1%. The market is pricing in nearly double the demonstrated revenue trajectory, sustained for a decade, with margin expansion on top.

None of this means Costco is a bad business — it may be the best-run retailer on Earth. It means the "safety premium" attached to it has never been larger, and the same staples rotation that makes Costco an AI-bubble hedge has inflated the entry price.

## Bottom line

Costco is the rare company that is genuinely built for any economy: countercyclical value perception, an affluent and renewing member base, a fee model that produces profit before a single item is scanned, and zero exposure to the AI capex cycle. The membership flywheel and the international runway give it another decade of dependable compounding.

The problem is purely arithmetic. At 48x earnings, the price already assumes Costco accelerates beyond its demonstrated trajectory and sustains it for ten years. This is a wonderful company at a price that discounts perfection — the kind of stock you own for the compounding, not for the value.
